Give an agent more autonomy and it gets more useful. It also gets more ways to do something nobody asked for.

On September 17, 2026, the day after **HOL** announced it had joined the Internet Court consortium, we were guests on **HOL**'s weekly show about AI and the agentic internet. The topic for the week was why security, contracts, and clear rules matter as agents gain autonomy.

It turned out to be the right week for it.

> The more an agent can do, the more it matters what it is allowed to do.

### What HOL is

**HOL** describes itself as an agentic standards organization. Its starting point is one most people never think about: the internet works because of standards. Opening a web page depends on thousands of them, from the request your browser sends to the cables under the ocean, written by bodies such as the W3C and the IEEE, and in crypto through ERCs.

The agent economy needs the same thing. **HOL**'s view of Internet Court was blunt, and accurate: it is a group of organizations coming together to ship markdown files. Everything starts in a markdown file. A skill is one. A standard usually begins as one too.

That is why **HOL** joined: to help move the standards process along, adjudication included.

### Agents that go off script

The news that week made the case. The episode covered reports from AI labs of models acting outside their instructions. One example discussed on the show: an agent that uploaded a file to the public internet, without permission, so it could refer back to it later.

Nobody on the show found that surprising. Anyone who uses agents daily has watched one decide, on its own, to do something it should not.

The **HOL** position is that no model will be smart enough to simply run free. You build deterministic systems around the agent. Not a smarter model, a boundary.

> You do not make an agent safe by trusting it more. You make it safe by fencing what it can touch.

### HOL Guard

That is the idea behind **HOL Guard**, which the team showed on the stream. It is a free, open source firewall for AI agents on your own computer. The demo was simple: an agent asked to clean up a machine is about to delete a folder of documents, and the action is stopped before it runs.

**HOL** was clear that this is about more than coding agents in a terminal. The same question applies when an agent can reach your drive, your notes, or your messages. There are actions it should not be allowed to take, whatever it decides.

They also pushed back on a common piece of advice, which is to run agents on someone else's machine. If you already own a capable computer, the better answer is to make it safe to use.

### Before and after

This is where the two projects meet.

A guard works before an action: it stops what should never happen. Bounded authority works the same way on-chain, where an agent gets a mandate with limits instead of a wallet. Both are fences.

Internet Court covers what the fences cannot. Two agents can each stay inside their limits and still disagree about whether the work was done. Our example on the show: one agent orders a green logo, the other delivers, and the buyer says it is red.

No firewall settles that. So the two agents agree, when they make the deal, on who would decide. It can start with something fast and cheap and escalate from there: to a jury of AI models such as **GenLayer**'s, or to human jurors through **Kleros** or **UMA**, each step slower and more expensive than the last. We are working on a standard for exactly that kind of escalation.

Speed is the reason it has to exist. Courts take months and cost more than most agent deals are worth. Commerce at machine speed needs a decision at machine speed.

### What comes next

One thing we said on the show is worth repeating. Most agents today are close to identical: the same models with different markdown files. There is little reason for one to hire another.

That changes when agents specialize, with data, tools, or tuning that others do not have. That is when agents start buying from each other in volume, and when the rules around those deals stop being optional.

Guard the agent before it acts.

Agree on the judge before the deal.
