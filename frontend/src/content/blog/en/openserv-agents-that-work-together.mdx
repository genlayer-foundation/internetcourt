For a while, an agent was one thing doing one job. Then three pieces arrived almost together: a way to pay, a way to be identified, and a way to learn new abilities without new code.

On July 14, 2026, **OpenServ** joined our launch panel, alongside **LI.FI** and **Kleros**, to describe what that combination unlocked.

> One agent is a tool. A group of agents that can hire each other is an economy.

### What OpenServ brings

**OpenServ** is an applied AI lab and a multi-agent orchestration platform. Builders create agents with its SDK, use agents built by other people, and combine them into a team that works on one task. The agents can come from any framework and live on any chain.

In the six-layer stack this is negotiation and coordination: agents splitting up work, routing tasks, and handing off to one another before any payment happens.

The team described the turning point plainly. **x402**, ERC-8004, and agent skills overlapped in time. Together they meant that any agent, or any group of agents, on **OpenServ** could offer its services for payment, publish an identity, and be discovered and hired by others. A workflow one person built can be chained with a workflow someone else built, with no code and no configuration.

Give the agent a funded wallet, and it decides what to buy and what to sell.

### Reasoning is the bottleneck

The second half of the conversation was about decisions.

A capable model produces better text. That is not the same as a better decision, made the same way every time. The **OpenServ** team argued that this is the real bottleneck for agents that handle money.

Their answer is a reasoning layer that sits between a builder's agent and the model. It is offered as a drop-in replacement for the usual model APIs, and it was in private beta at the time of the stream.

Two ideas from it are worth keeping, whatever tools you use.

Move what is deterministic into code. If a rule says "buy only if risk is under 80%," do not ask a language model to do the arithmetic. Compute it.

Keep an auditable record. When something goes wrong, you want the exact step and the exact decision, not a paragraph of explanation after the fact.

> Better text is not a better decision.

### Where Internet Court comes in

Both ideas line up with how disputes work.

A contract between agents should make everything that can be checked mechanically a mechanical check, and save judgment for the part that needs it. And when a deal is disputed, the record of what each agent actually did is the evidence. An agent with an auditable trail is an agent that can defend its work.

Orchestration also multiplies counterparties. A team of five agents hiring three outside services is eight parties, not two. Each handoff is a small deal that can go wrong.

### In the skill

The Internet Court skill includes all five of **OpenServ**'s official skills, covering its agent SDK, its client, and multi-agent workflows. The workflows register an ERC-8004 identity for the agents involved, so an agent arrives at a deal with an on-chain identity already in place.

### One thing to try

Their advice to builders: pay attention to the reasoning process, not just to which model you call.

Agents working together is the easy part to picture.

Agents answering for their decisions is what makes it trustworthy.
