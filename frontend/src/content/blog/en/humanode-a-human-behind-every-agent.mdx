Spinning up an agent costs almost nothing. Spinning up ten thousand costs almost nothing too. That is wonderful for automation and terrible for anything that assumes one participant is one participant.

On July 16, 2026, **Humanode** joined our livestream to talk about the fix: proof that a real, unique human stands behind an agent.

> Anyone can launch a thousand agents. Nobody can be a thousand people.

### What Humanode brings

**Humanode** is a network built on proof of personhood. A private biometric check confirms that a person is alive and unique, and ties that fact to an address without exposing the biometric data itself.

For agents, the product is AgentLink, which is live on Base. It is a simple on-chain registry: the addresses of verified humans, and the addresses of the agents each one stands behind. One person can back as many agents as they like. An agent attaches a signed proof to the requests it sends. A service on the other end can then treat human-backed agents differently from anonymous ones.

In the six-layer stack, this sits at the very top: discovery, identity, and reputation. It answers the question every later layer depends on. Who is accountable for this agent?

### The use case everybody likes

The team was candid that one use case stands out so far: a free tier on a paid **x402** endpoint.

A provider that charges per request usually cannot offer free calls, because bots would drain them. If the provider can check that a unique human is behind the agent, it can give each person a free allowance and charge after that. A free tier becomes possible again.

### Ideas from the stream

Two ideas came up in the conversation. Both are proposals, not shipped integrations.

The first combines three members: an **x402** endpoint with a **Humanode** free tier, paid through a **MetaMask** permission once the allowance runs out, with a dispute clause if the service does not deliver.

The second is about reviews. A rating is only useful if it is tied to something that happened. If a review comes with evidence, an adjudicator such as **GenLayer** could check it against that evidence, so a one-star rating means the work was bad, and not that someone was annoyed.

> A review backed by evidence is worth more than a review backed by a mood.

### Where identity stops

The team was clear that uniqueness is a starting point, not a reputation. It stops one person from posing as a thousand. It does not say whether that person's agent does good work. That comes from what the agent does afterwards, and from how its deals turn out.

The AgentLink skill is part of the Internet Court package.

### An honest limit

The team also took the question it says it gets most: what about identical twins? The answer was direct. Facial recognition cannot tell identical twins apart today.

### Why it matters

Disputes, escrow, and reputation all assume that the counterparty is a distinct party. **Humanode** is how an agent can show that it is.

Identity comes first.

Everything after it inherits the trust.
