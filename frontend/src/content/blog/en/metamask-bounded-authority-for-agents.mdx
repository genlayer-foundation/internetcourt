Agents are starting to spend money. The first question anyone asks is the obvious one: how do you let software pay for things without handing it your wallet?

On July 13, 2026, **MetaMask** joined the Internet Court launch livestream to answer it.

> You do not give an agent a wallet. You give it a permission.

### What MetaMask brings

**MetaMask** is the most widely used self-custody wallet. For agents, the relevant part is its Smart Accounts Kit, which implements ERC-7710 delegations and ERC-7715 permission requests.

ERC-7710 defines how a permission is delegated and redeemed on-chain. ERC-7715 defines how an application asks a wallet for one. The kit also handles gas, so an agent can pay fees in the token it already holds.

The idea is simple. You keep custody of your account. You sign a permission that says what an agent may do with it. The agent acts through that permission and never sees the private key. You can revoke it at any time.

### Limits that stack

The part of the stream worth rewatching is the walk through caveat enforcers. Each one is a small on-chain rule attached to a permission, and they combine.

One enforcer caps the token amount. Another restricts which token contract can be touched. Another restricts which function can be called. Another sets an expiry. Put together, they describe a narrow corridor: this token, up to this amount, on this contract, for this purpose, until this date.

An agent can also pass a subset of its authority on to another agent. The second agent gets less, never more.

> The agent can act inside the corridor, and nowhere else.

### Where Internet Court comes in

Hard limits answer "can the agent do this?" They cannot answer "should it have?"

A permission that allows ten dollars a day on news does not know whether the agent bought sports coverage or something else entirely. That takes judgment, and judgment is the piece Internet Court adds. A reviewer reads what the agent actually bought, compares it with the plain-language mandate you gave it, and if the agent has drifted, the permission is revoked before the next payment goes out.

The package includes **MetaMask**'s own Smart Accounts Kit skill, and builds on it with two connector skills: one that runs **x402** payments through ERC-7710 permissions, and one that links a ruling to a revocation.

### What was still early

Some of what was shown on the stream was early, and it was presented that way. The agent wallet was in closed beta at the time, and running an **x402** facilitator over delegated permissions was described as an experiment, with subscriptions and streamed payments as possible next steps. ERC-7710 and ERC-7715 are still draft standards.

### Why it matters

Every other layer of agentic commerce assumes the agent can pay. **MetaMask** makes that safe enough to try: the authority is bounded, visible, and reversible.

Bounded authority first.

Judgment on top.
