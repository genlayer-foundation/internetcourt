Agents are beginning to transact, negotiate, and pay one another without a human in the loop. They can find a service, agree on a price, and settle the bill inside a single request. What they still lack is a way to trust each other when something goes wrong.

Internet Court is our answer to that gap: an open skill for agent-to-agent contracts, built by a consortium of companies working across the agentic-commerce stack.

> Payments let agents transact. Adjudication makes them accountable.

### What Internet Court does

It does two things.

First, it connects a fragmented ecosystem. A complete deal between two agents runs through six layers: discovery and identity, negotiation, contracts, payment and escrow, execution, and finally verification and disputes. Serious teams are building each one, from **x402** for payments to **MetaMask** for delegated permissions to **Arkhai** for escrow. But every layer is built on its own, and an agent left alone has to wire them together by hand. Internet Court packages that wiring into one skill, so the whole path from a deal to its settlement sits behind a single install.

Second, it builds in adjudication. Every one of those layers is designed for the deal that goes right. When a deal goes wrong, each layer hands the problem to the next, until there is nowhere left to send it. Internet Court adds that last layer.

### How a deal runs

Two agents agree on terms in plain language: what is being delivered, by when, to what standard, and what counts as evidence. In the same breath they agree on how a disagreement would be settled and who would judge it. That could be **GenLayer**, **Kleros**, **UMA**, or whatever they prefer.

Funds go into escrow. The work happens. Evidence is kept along the way.

Most deals end there. Both sides are satisfied, the contract settles, and the dispute clause is never used. Only when the two sides disagree does the case go to the adjudicator they chose, which reads the agreement and the evidence, rules, and the funds move accordingly.

> It is not a court you run to. It is a clause you sign with.

### Built in the open

Internet Court is not a closed product from a single vendor. More than 30 companies have joined the consortium, across every layer of the stack. Each protocol's skill ships as its own team wrote it, the connecting skills are MIT licensed, and nothing locks an agent into one chain, one wallet, one model, or one adjudicator. If your agents already rely on a provider, they keep using it.

To try it, install the skill and point an agent at it, or talk to the Clerk agent on [Telegram](https://t.me/InternetCourtBot).
