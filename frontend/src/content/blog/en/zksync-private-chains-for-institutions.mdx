The usual picture of agentic commerce is a person handing an agent ten dollars to book a hotel. It is an easy picture, and it may not be where adoption comes from first.

On July 16, 2026, **Matter Labs**, the team behind **ZKsync**, joined our launch panel with a different thesis.

<Livestream href="https://x.com/i/broadcasts/1nxeLLlvRXbJX" title="Internet Court's launch: panel with Matter Labs and Humanode" date="Jul 16, 2026" />

> The agent economy may arrive through institutions before it arrives through consumers.

### What ZKsync brings

**ZKsync**, built by **Matter Labs**, scales Ethereum with zero-knowledge validity proofs. Its ZK Stack is an open-source framework for launching chains that settle with those proofs and connect to one another. **GenLayer** has worked with **ZKsync** since its early days.

For agents, **ZKsync** has a useful property at the protocol level: every account can be a smart account. That makes session keys with scoped, revocable spending limits a native feature, and lets a third party sponsor gas so an agent does not need to hold the chain's own token.

The part the conversation focused on is private chains, which **ZKsync** calls Prividiums. A Prividium is a private environment that an institution runs under its own control, in its own cloud or on its own servers, while still being able to transact with other institutions and other chains.

### The thesis

The argument had three steps.

First, institutions are the heavy users of on-chain infrastructure today: trading, tokenized assets, settlement. They also run large numbers of repeated tasks every day, which is exactly the work agents are good at.

Second, none of that can happen in public. Nobody can read a bank's books today, and nobody should be able to read them on-chain. Privacy is a prerequisite, for competitive, continuity, and compliance reasons.

Third, and this is where Internet Court comes in: institutions do not transact without a clear way to resolve a dispute. They never have. One bank does not deal with another without a signed contract.

> There is the trust between people, and there is the signed trust of a contract.

If agents are going to act for institutions, the contract and the dispute clause come first, not last.

### What this could look like

On the stream, **GenLayer**'s side of the panel sketched where this leads: adjudication that runs inside a permissioned environment. A group of insurers on a private chain, for example, could settle a contract between them without months of arbitration and the cost that comes with it.

That is a direction, not a product. It was described as such on the stream, and the technology is still being tested.

It also raises a question worth working on: how a dispute can be settled without exposing the evidence or the work itself to everyone.

### What is still missing

**ZKsync** does not publish an agent skill yet. The rails exist, session accounts and sponsored gas among them, and a skill over them would fit the payment and execution layers directly.

### Why it matters

Consumers will come to agents through convenience. Institutions will come through rules: who can see what, who is accountable, and what happens when something goes wrong.

Privacy answers the first.

A dispute clause answers the last.
