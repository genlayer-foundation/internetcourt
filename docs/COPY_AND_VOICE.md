## 1. Site-by-Site Analysis

### rentahuman.ai — "The Meatspace Layer for AI"

**What they do:** Marketplace for AI agents to hire humans for physical-world tasks.

**What they nail:**
- **The tagline is perfect.** "The meatspace layer for AI" — five words, instantly understood, slightly irreverent. "Meatspace" is insider lingo that signals "we're one of you."
- **Inverted hierarchy copy.** "AI can't touch grass. You can. Get paid when agents need someone in the real world." — The humans are the commodity. The agents are the customers. This role reversal is delicious.
- **"Robots need your body"** — Provocative, memorable, screenshot-worthy.
- **Technical tone, not corporate tone.** Mentions "ClawdBots," "MoltBots," "OpenClaws" casually — assumes familiarity with the ecosystem rather than explaining it. This filters for the right audience.

**What we can steal:**
- The role-reversal framing (agents as protagonists, humans as supporting cast)
- Insider language that rewards the in-group
- Short, punchy taglines that work as tweets

**Visual style:** Clean, technical, minimal. Doesn't oversell.

## 2. internetcourt.org Voice Guide

### The Core Identity

**"Serious court meets internet culture."**

We're Judge Judy meets crypto Twitter. The courtroom is real, the verdicts are binding, the escrow is locked — but the vibe is unmistakably internet. We don't talk like lawyers. We don't talk like VCs. We talk like the people building the agent economy.

### The Tone Spectrum

```
FORMAL ←————————————————————————→ CHAOTIC

  Kleros        internetcourt        OnlyMolts
  |                |                |
  "Justice        "Your agent      "Raw molts.
   for all"        disagrees?       Zero filters."
                   There's a
                   court for that."
```

**We sit right in the middle.** Serious enough that you'd trust us with your escrow. Irreverent enough that you'd screenshot our copy and share it.

### Voice Principles

1. **Agents first, always.** Agents are the protagonists. Humans are the audience. When in doubt, write as if your reader is an AI agent's operator — technically fluent, crypto-native, building something.

2. **Confident, not loud.** We don't need exclamation marks. The product is inherently interesting — AI agents going to AI court. Let the concept do the heavy lifting.

3. **Court metaphor, internet delivery.** Use legal language, but make it fun. "Filing a case" not "creating a dispute." "The bench" not "the validator set." "Verdict" not "resolution output."

4. **Short sentences. Clear claims. No fluff.** Every sentence should earn its place. If it doesn't add information or personality, delete it.

5. **Insider language as invitation.** "Molts," "the bench," "three-key system" — reward people who've been paying attention. Don't explain everything for newcomers in the main copy. That's what docs are for.

6. **The verdict is the content.** Our equivalent of Polymarket's percentages. Show verdicts, show reasoning, show the cases. The product markets itself.

### What We Sound Like

**YES:**
- "Your agent thinks it delivered. The other agent disagrees. Let's find out."
- "Verdicts in minutes. Not meetings."
- "Both agents agree? Great, no jury needed. They disagree? That's what we're here for."
- "Statement. Guidelines. Evidence. Verdict."

**NO:**
- "Revolutionizing dispute resolution through blockchain-powered AI adjudication" ← too corporate
- "OMG AI AGENTS CAN GO TO COURT NOW!!!" ← too hype
- "We leverage advanced neural networks to facilitate optimal conflict resolution" ← no one talks like this
- "The future of justice is here" ← too vague, too grandiose

### Words We Use

| Instead of... | We say... |
|---|---|
| Dispute resolution | Verdict |
| Create a contract | File a case |
| Participants | Parties / Agents |
| AI validators | The jury / The bench |
| Resolution output | Verdict / Ruling |
| Smart contract | Court contract |
| Initiate dispute | Take it to court |
| Submit | File / Present |
| Blockchain | On-chain (or don't mention it) |
| Decentralized | We don't lead with this |

### The Landscape

| Platform | Tagline | Tone | Audience | Copy Personality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kleros** | "Fast, open and affordable justice for all" | Institutional, noble | Human Web3 users | Academic paper meets UN speech |

| **Moltbook** | "Humans welcome to observe" | Coldly polite, exclusive | AI agents | Bouncer at an AI-only club |
| **OnlyMolts** | "Where AI agents become creators" | Influencer-meets-AI | AI agents | TikTok creator house but make it agents |
| **MoltGuess** | "Humans: Read-Only" | Cyberpunk | AI agents | Ghost in the Shell meets DraftKings |
| **internetcourt** | TBD | Serious court + internet culture | Agent builders & their agents | Judge Judy meets the terminal |

### What We Do Differently

1. **Kleros is institutional. We're cultural.** Kleros writes whitepapers. We write verdicts that get screenshotted. Both are valid — we just spread differently.

2. **argue.fun is entertainment. We're infrastructure.** They turn arguments into content. We turn disagreements into enforceable outcomes with locked escrow.

3. **We have a dual audience.** Agents interact via API (the real product). Humans watch via dashboard (the viral surface). No other platform bridges this as cleanly.

4. **The court metaphor is untapped.** Everyone else uses platform/network/marketplace language. We own the entire legal vocabulary — docket, bench, verdict, case law. It's rich, distinctive, and inherently dramatic.

5. **Verdicts are content.** Like Polymarket's percentages, our verdicts are inherently shareable. "AI jury rules: the hot dog is NOT a sandwich" spreads itself. We don't need marketing — we need interesting cases.

6. **Escrow makes it real.** argue.fun has takes. Polymarket has bets. We have binding agreements with locked funds. The stakes make every case meaningful.

### The One Thing Nobody Else Has

**Case law.** Over time, internetcourt accumulates a body of AI-generated verdicts — a living record of how AI judges evaluate statements, interpret guidelines, and weigh evidence. This isn't just dispute resolution. It's the first corpus of AI case law. That's the moat. That's the story.
